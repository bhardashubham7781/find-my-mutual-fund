How to Compare Indian Mutual Funds by Sector Exposure

A mutual fund's sector allocation shows how its disclosed portfolio is distributed across industries. Comparing this exposure can help you understand overlap between schemes and find portfolios that fit specific screening criteria—such as a maximum allocation to IT, banking or energy.

Find My Mutual Fund is a free tool for screening selected Indian mutual funds by disclosed sector exposure. You choose the rules; the screener compares portfolios against those rules and shows the gaps instead of making a buy or sell recommendation. What is mutual fund sector exposure?

Sector exposure is the share of a portfolio allocated to companies classified in a particular industry. A diversified equity scheme might hold companies across banking, information technology, healthcare, consumer goods, energy and industrials. Two funds in the same category can still have different sector mixes.

Sector allocation is only one view of a fund. It does not replace analysis of the underlying companies, valuation, concentration, fund strategy, costs, risk or your investment horizon. A low allocation to one sector does not by itself make a scheme better.

How to find mutual funds by sector allocation

  1. Choose a sector. Start with an exposure you want to limit or require, such as IT or Banking.
  2. Set a rule. Exclude the sector by setting a 0% maximum, specify another maximum, or set a minimum exposure. Leave sectors you do not care about unrestricted.
  3. Apply more than one rule if needed. For example, set a maximum for IT and a separate minimum for Pharma & Healthcare. The tool compares each fund against all selected rules.
  4. Filter the results. Narrow the comparison by fund category or asset management company (AMC), or search for a scheme by name.
  5. Read the details. Check the allocation breakdown, source month and any quality notes. Confirm the numbers using the relevant AMC's original monthly portfolio disclosure or factsheet.

Find funds with low IT or banking exposure

To screen for funds whose reported IT allocation is zero, set the IT rule to exclude the sector or cap it at 0%. To limit exposure rather than eliminate it, set a maximum such as 5% or 10%. These are examples of user-selected screening thresholds, not investment recommendations.

The same method works for Banking, Financial Services, Pharma & Healthcare, Auto, FMCG, Energy/Oil & Gas, Power, Metals & Mining, Capital Goods, Construction/Infra, Telecom, Consumer Durables, Realty, Chemicals and Others. Source classifications and the screener's standard sector mapping may not be identical to every AMC's own labels, so check the original portfolio disclosure for important decisions.

How to interpret the sector-match score

The screener calculates the gap between each fund's reported sector exposure and your chosen limits. Lower scores indicate a closer match to the selected rules. A score of zero means all of those chosen rules are met; it does not mean the fund has low risk, strong future returns or suitability for a particular investor.

Compare the full breakdown, not just the rank. Consider whether the fund's category and overall holdings match the purpose of your research. For investment decisions, review official scheme documents and consider advice appropriate to your circumstances.

Which sectors can you compare?

The screener currently uses 16 broad labels: IT; Banking; Financial Services; Pharma & Healthcare; Auto; FMCG; Energy/Oil & Gas; Power; Metals & Mining; Capital Goods; Construction/Infra; Telecom; Consumer Durables; Realty; Chemicals; and Others.

“Others” can include industries that do not fit the screener's current standard categories. As a result, a mapped sector may not correspond one-to-one with the AMC's own presentation. Use the original holdings and sector labels as the reference when investigating a discrepancy.

Data coverage and checking the source

The current public beta uses monthly AMC portfolio disclosures and contains selected scheme-matched portfolios from SBI Mutual Fund, ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund and HDFC Mutual Fund. This release is based on September 2026 disclosures and covers 84 schemes. It is not a complete catalogue of every Indian mutual fund; other schemes and AMCs will be included only as the dataset is expanded and verified.

Portfolio information may be delayed. Before relying on a figure, open the relevant AMC's latest factsheet or monthly portfolio disclosure, confirm the scheme name and plan, and check that the disclosure month matches the data shown by the screener.

Frequently asked questions

Can I find mutual funds with no IT exposure?

You can set the IT maximum to 0% and screen the currently covered schemes against that condition. Always verify the result against the fund's original disclosure; indirect economic exposure and classification differences may not be captured.

Does a lower sector-match score mean a fund is better?

No. The score measures only how closely a fund matches your selected sector rules. It does not predict returns or measure every aspect of investment risk.

Is the screener's fund list complete?

No. This is a limited public beta. It currently includes selected portfolios from three AMCs, and both coverage and sector mapping can change as the data is reviewed.

How often is sector data updated?

The tool uses monthly AMC portfolio disclosures, which may be published after month-end and may be delayed in the screener. Check the displayed source month and the AMC's latest official disclosure.
