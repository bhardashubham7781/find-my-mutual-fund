Data & scoring methodology

This page explains the portfolio sources, standard sector mapping, scheme matching and rule-based score used by Find My Mutual Fund. The aim is to make the screener's coverage and limitations visible so you can verify the original disclosure.

1. Portfolio data sources

The current public beta uses selected scheme-matched monthly portfolio disclosures obtained from the following asset management companies. These are links to the official source portals so you can check original records and newer disclosures.

  • SBI Mutual Fund — portfolio disclosures
  • HDFC Mutual Fund — monthly portfolio disclosures
  • ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund — monthly portfolio disclosures
  • AMFI — Association of Mutual Funds in India

The current release contains 38 selected SBI Mutual Fund schemes, 45 ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund schemes and 1 HDFC Mutual Fund scheme. It is a limited sample, not a complete catalogue of every Indian mutual fund. Additional AMCs and schemes will be added only after their source files, mappings and scheme matches are reviewed.

2. Scheme identification and inclusion

Portfolio names are compared with a review list of scheme names and AMFI scheme codes to match each holding file or workbook to a scheme record. Records that do not have an acceptable scheme match are not published in the screener. The plan, option and scheme category should always be checked against the AMC's original record.

The processing workflow also checks for missing holdings and whether the reported portfolio percentages reconcile to an expected range around 100%. These automated checks help catch parsing and completeness issues; they do not prove that every row was classified correctly or replace an independent comparison with the official factsheet.

3. Sector mapping

Different AMC documents can use different industry labels. The screener maps source industry labels into 16 broader categories: IT, Banking, Financial Services, Pharma & Healthcare, Auto, FMCG, Energy/Oil & Gas, Power, Metals & Mining, Capital Goods, Construction/Infra, Telecom, Consumer Durables, Realty, Chemicals and Others.

Some source industries do not have a direct one-to-one counterpart in this standard list. They may be grouped under “Others”. This makes the category set usable across sources, but it also means the screener's labels may not exactly match the AMC's own sector presentation. Check the original holdings and sector labels when the distinction matters.

4. How the distance score works

Users choose the sector rules. For a maximum rule, only the percentage points above the selected maximum contribute to the score. For a minimum rule, only the percentage points below the minimum contribute. For an excluded sector, its reported exposure contributes to the score if it is above zero. Sector gaps are added together across the rules selected by the user.

A lower score means a closer match to those selected conditions. A zero score means all chosen rules are met. It is not a rating of investment quality, a measure of total portfolio risk, or a forecast of returns. Leaving a sector at “No preference” means it does not affect the score.

5. Update timing and limitations

The current dataset is based on September 2026 disclosures. AMC disclosures may be published after month-end, and the data served by this site can lag the latest files. The scheme list and mapping rules may change as the project adds sources and performs further reviews.
